Module 7: Production Planning (PP)

For beginners: Production Planning (PP) is the part of SAP that answers: “What do we need to make or buy, when, and how much?” It uses demand (e.g. sales orders or forecasts) and master data—BOM (Bill of Material: what components go into a product), routing (what steps to perform), and work center (where and with what capacity)—to create planned orders and then production orders. When you execute a production order, the system issues components from stock (MM), records labor and machine time, and receives the finished product into stock; costs flow to CO. Key terms: MRP (Material Requirements Planning: the run that calculates net requirements and creates planned orders), production order, and confirmation (posting actual time and quantity).

SAP PP covers demand management, MRP, production execution, and capacity planning. It uses master data (BOM, work center, routing) and demand to generate planned orders and production orders, which then consume materials (MM) and post costs (CO).

Purpose and scope

PP ensures that production is planned and executed in sync with demand and capacity. Key sub-areas: Demand Management (sales forecasts, planned independent requirements); MRP (net requirement = demand − available stock − open supply; lot sizing; planned orders); BOM and routing (how to produce); work center (where and with what capacity); production orders (discrete manufacturing) or repetitive manufacturing; capacity planning and leveling. PP-DS extends planning with finite scheduling. Integration with SD (demand from orders), MM (procurement and stock), and CO (costing and settlement) is central.

In plain terms: A BOM says “to make 1 unit of product X you need 2 units of A and 1 unit of B.” The routing says “operation 10 at work center WC01, 30 minutes; operation 20 at WC02, 15 minutes.” MRP looks at demand (e.g. 100 units of X needed) and current stock, then creates planned orders or purchase requisitions. Converting a planned order to a production order is the start of execution; the order then drives material issues and confirmations.

Features in detail

  • Bill of Material (BOM): List of components (materials and quantities) required to produce one unit of a finished or semi-finished product. Multiple BOMs per material (by plant, alternative). STPO (item), MAST (material–BOM link).
  • Work center and routing: Work center = machine or labor center (capacity, cost). Routing = sequence of operations (work center, standard times). Used for capacity planning and costing.
  • MRP: Net requirement = demand − available stock − open supply. MRP run creates planned orders (or purchase requisitions for externally procured materials). MD04 (stock/requirements list), MD02 (MRP run for one material).
  • Production order (PP-PI-PRO): Convert planned order to production order (CO01/CO40). Order has operations (from routing), component requirements (from BOM). Release → print/confirm → goods issue (components) → confirmation (labor/machine) → goods receipt (finished product) → settle to CO.
  • Repetitive manufacturing: Simplified flow: production version, backflush (components and product posted at period end or at confirmation).

Data plane and control plane

Data plane: Production order (AFKO header, AFPO operations, RESB component reservations), BOM (STPO, MAST), work center (CRHD, CRTX), planned orders, MRP results (reservations, requisitions). Control plane: Plant, MRP areas, MRP types and lot-sizing; work center and routing configuration; order type and settlement rules (SPRO). See Module 1.

Deployment and integration

Deployment: On‑premise and S/4HANA Cloud. Integration: Demand from SD (sales orders) and demand management; procurement from MM (purchase requisition → PO); goods movement (MM); costing and settlement to CO/FI; IBP for advanced planning (Module 19).

Example: From sales order to production order and goods receipt

Finished product FG-100 has a BOM: 2× RAW-A, 1× RAW-B. Sales order creates demand for 10 FG-100.

  1. MRP (MD02 or MD40): MRP runs for FG-100. Net requirement 10; no stock. System creates planned order for 10 FG-100 and (via BOM explosion) dependent requirements for 20 RAW-A and 10 RAW-B. If RAW-A/RAW-B are procured, MRP creates purchase requisitions.
  2. Convert to production order (CO01): Planned order is converted to production order 1000000123. Order has operations (from routing) and component list (from BOM). Order is released.
  3. Goods issue (MIGO or CO11N): Components 20 RAW-A and 10 RAW-B are issued to the order (stock decrease). Confirmation (CO11N): Labor and machine time confirmed. Goods receipt: 10 FG-100 received into stock. Order is settled to CO (and optionally capitalized to product cost).

Result: FG-100 in stock to fulfill the sales order; component stock reduced; costs in CO.

Important T-codes and concepts

MRP: MD04 (stock/requirements list), MD02 (single-item MRP), MD40 (multi-level). Production order: CO01 (create), CO02 (change), COR1 (create from planned order). Concepts: Planned order vs production order; BOM explosion; capacity planning; settlement of production order to CO (product cost, variance).

📝 Module Quiz

Q1: MRP in PP stands for:

  • A) Material Requirements Planning
  • B) Master Record Planning
  • C) Only SD
  • D) Only FI
Correct: A
MRP = Material Requirements Planning.

Q2: BOM is:

  • A) Bill of material
  • B) Bank order management
  • C) Only CO
  • D) Only MM document
Correct: A
BOM = Bill of material (components for production).

Q3: Production order costs settle to:

  • A) Only SD
  • B) CO (and FI via settlement)
  • C) Only MM
  • D) Only QM
Correct: B
Production orders are costed in CO and can settle to CO/FI.

Q4: What does the stock/requirements list (MD04) show?

  • A) Only FI documents
  • B) Demand, receipts, stock, and planned orders for a material
  • C) Only SD billing
  • D) Only vendor master
Correct: B
MD04 shows all demand, supply, and stock for one material in one plant.
