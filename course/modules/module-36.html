Module 36: SAP Sustainability / ESG

For beginners: ESG stands for Environmental, Social, and Governance. Regulators, investors, and customers increasingly require companies to measure and report carbon footprint, energy use, social metrics (e.g. labour, safety), and governance. SAP’s sustainability solutions help you collect sustainability data from operations (e.g. from FI, MM, travel), calculate emissions (e.g. product carbon footprint), and report for frameworks such as CSRD, GRI, or CDP. Key products include SAP Sustainability Control Tower, SAP Product Footprint Management (PFM), and embedded capabilities in S/4HANA (e.g. environmental accounting). Key terms: carbon footprint, scope 1/2/3 emissions, CSRD, product carbon footprint (PCF).

SAP’s sustainability portfolio spans cloud applications for ESG reporting and analytics, product footprint calculation, and operational data collection. These connect to S/4HANA, SAP BW/4HANA, and SAP Datasphere so that transactional data (purchases, travel, energy, waste) can feed into sustainability metrics and disclosures.

Purpose and scope

Environmental: Track and report greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions—Scope 1 (direct, e.g. company vehicles, on-site fuel), Scope 2 (indirect, e.g. purchased electricity), Scope 3 (value chain: purchased goods, business travel, waste). Calculate product carbon footprint (PCF) for products. Manage environmental compliance (waste, water, emissions to air). Social: Labour metrics, health and safety (EHS can feed in), diversity, supply chain social criteria. Governance: Compliance, ethics, risk; overlaps with GRC (Module 32). Reporting is aligned to frameworks such as CSRD (EU Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive), GRI, CDP, and TCFD. Data can come from S/4HANA (e.g. travel via Concur, procurement via Ariba, energy consumption), manual input, or external sources.

In plain terms: Scope 1 = emissions you directly cause (e.g. gas in your boilers). Scope 2 = emissions from energy you buy (e.g. electricity). Scope 3 = everything else in your value chain (suppliers’ emissions, business travel, product use and end-of-life). Most of a company’s footprint is often Scope 3, but it’s the hardest to measure; SAP helps by linking procurement, travel, and product data to emission factors.

Features in detail

  • SAP Sustainability Control Tower: Central ESG data model and reporting. Connects to source systems (S/4HANA, Concur, Ariba, spreadsheets). Supports data collection workflows, emission factor management, and report templates for CSRD, GRI, CDP. Dashboards for carbon, energy, water, waste, and social KPIs. Often deployed on SAP BTP with Datasphere for data integration.
  • SAP Product Footprint Management (PFM): Calculates product carbon footprint (PCF) at product level. Uses bill of materials (BOM), procurement data, and emission factors (e.g. per kg material, per kWh). Can run in batch or near real time. Output: PCF per product/SKU for labelling, customer requests, or internal reduction targets. Integrates with S/4HANA (MM, CO-PA) and PLM.
  • Environmental accounting in S/4HANA: Record environmental quantities (e.g. energy consumption, waste, emissions) alongside financial postings. Enables “green ledger” view: which cost centres or products have which environmental impact. Feeds into sustainability reporting and PFM.
  • Greenhouse Gas (GHG) emission management: Define emission factors (e.g. kg CO2e per kWh, per litre fuel, per tonne material). Allocate emissions to cost centres, projects, or products. Track over time for reduction targets. Support for market-based and location-based Scope 2.
  • Supply chain sustainability: Assess supplier sustainability (questionnaires, certifications). Link procurement spend to Scope 3 (purchased goods and services). Integrate with SAP Ariba and S/4HANA MM for supplier and material data.

Data plane and control plane

Data plane: Sustainability data lives in cloud (Control Tower, PFM) and/or S/4HANA (environmental accounting). Source data: FI/CO (cost centre consumption), MM (purchases, materials), Concur (travel), Ariba (supplier spend). Emission factors and allocation rules are master data. Aggregated metrics (e.g. total Scope 1/2/3 per period) are calculated and stored for reporting. Control plane: Configuration of emission factors, activity types (e.g. kWh, litres), organisational assignment (company, cost centre, product). Report templates and framework mappings (CSRD, GRI) are configured in Control Tower. See Module 1 for SPRO in S/4HANA.

Deployment and integration

Deployment: SAP Sustainability Control Tower and SAP Product Footprint Management are cloud offerings (often on BTP). Environmental accounting and some sustainability-relevant master data are in S/4HANA (on‑premise or cloud). Integration: Control Tower and PFM connect to S/4HANA (OData, APIs, or via Datasphere), SAP Concur (travel), SAP Ariba (procurement), and optionally SAP EHS for safety and environmental compliance. Datasphere can consolidate data from multiple sources for a single sustainability data layer.

Example: Scope 2 emissions and product carbon footprint

Scope 2: Company records electricity consumption per cost centre in S/4HANA (e.g. from meters or allocations). In Sustainability Control Tower, emission factors are defined (e.g. 0.35 kg CO2e per kWh for location-based; 0.28 for market-based if green tariff). System multiplies consumption × factor and aggregates by company and period. Report shows Scope 2 in tonnes CO2e for the year, with year-on-year comparison.

Product carbon footprint: Product “Widget A” has a BOM: 2 kg steel, 0.5 kg plastic, 1 kWh assembly energy. PFM has emission factors: steel 2.5 kg CO2e/kg, plastic 4 kg CO2e/kg, electricity 0.35 kg CO2e/kWh. PCF = (2×2.5) + (0.5×4) + (1×0.35) = 5 + 2 + 0.35 = 7.35 kg CO2e per unit. This figure is stored and can be used for customer requests, packaging labels, or internal reduction initiatives.

Key concepts and reporting

Emission factors are stored as master data (per activity type and optionally region/year). Double materiality (financial and impact) is required under CSRD: report how sustainability affects the company and how the company affects environment and society. SAP supports both. Product footprint can be calculated at batch level for “green” variants (e.g. recycled content) using different factors. Integration with SAP IBP and S/4HANA allows “what-if” scenarios (e.g. change supplier or transport mode and recalculate footprint).

📝 Module Quiz

Q1: What are Scope 1, 2, and 3 emissions?

  • A) Three different companies
  • B) Scope 1 = direct; Scope 2 = indirect from energy; Scope 3 = value chain (e.g. purchased goods, travel)
  • C) Only Scope 1 matters
  • D) Only for finance
Correct: B
Scope 1/2/3 is the standard GHG Protocol classification for where emissions occur.

Q2: What does SAP Product Footprint Management (PFM) do?

  • A) Only financial reporting
  • B) Calculates product-level carbon footprint using BOM, procurement, and emission factors
  • C) Only HR
  • D) Only warehouse
Correct: B
PFM computes the carbon footprint per product/SKU for labelling and reporting.

Q3: CSRD refers to:

  • A) A programming language
  • B) EU Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive; mandates detailed ESG reporting
  • C) A SAP module
  • D) Only Scope 1
Correct: B
CSRD is the EU regulation driving much of the need for structured ESG data and reporting.

Q4: Which SAP solutions typically feed sustainability reporting?

  • A) Only FI
  • B) S/4HANA (FI, CO, MM), Concur (travel), Ariba (procurement), and environmental accounting
  • C) Only SD
  • D) Only PM
Correct: B
Sustainability data is sourced from operations (FI, CO, MM), travel (Concur), and procurement (Ariba), plus environmental quantities.
