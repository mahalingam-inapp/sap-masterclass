Module 9: Plant Maintenance (PM)

For beginners: Plant Maintenance (PM) is the part of SAP that manages maintaining and repairing equipment and facilities—machines, pumps, buildings, etc. You maintain master data: functional locations (hierarchical places, e.g. “Building A → Line 1 → Machine 3”) and equipment (individual assets like a specific pump). Preventive maintenance is planned in advance (e.g. “inspect every 30 days”); corrective maintenance is triggered by a failure or defect. A maintenance order is like a work order: it has operations (labor, external service), materials (spare parts from MM), and costs that post to CO. Notifications record defects or requests; they can be converted to orders. PM integrates with MM (spare parts) and CO (costs).

SAP PM covers preventive and corrective maintenance. Maintenance orders are created from notifications or maintenance plans; they consume materials (MM) and post costs to CO (and optionally to FI for capitalizations).

Purpose and scope

PM ensures that assets are maintained on schedule (preventive) or repaired when failures occur (corrective). Key sub-areas: Master data (functional location, equipment); Maintenance plans (recurring tasks); Notifications (defect or activity report); Maintenance orders (work order with operations, materials, and cost); Capacity and planning; Integration with MM (spare parts, procurement) and CO (cost collection and settlement). QM quality notifications can trigger PM notifications for defect analysis.

In plain terms: A maintenance plan says “every 30 days, do task X on equipment E.” The system then generates call objects (e.g. a maintenance order or notification) when the cycle is due. A notification is raised when someone reports a problem; you can then create a maintenance order from it, execute the work (issue materials, confirm labor), and settle the order to a cost center or asset so that costs are tracked correctly.

Features in detail

  • Functional location (IFLO): Hierarchical structure (e.g. Site → Building → Line → Machine). Used to organize where equipment is installed and to plan maintenance at a location level.
  • Equipment (EQUI): Individual technical object (e.g. pump, motor). Can be installed at a functional location. Equipment history (orders, notifications) is tracked.
  • Maintenance plan: Defines recurring tasks (e.g. “inspect pump every 30 days” or “every 1,000 operating hours”). System generates call objects (maintenance order or notification) based on cycle.
  • Notification (QMEL/IW21): Reports a defect or request for maintenance. Can be created manually or from QM. Notification can be converted to maintenance order.
  • Maintenance order (IW31/IW32): Order has operations (labor, external service), material components (spare parts), and cost. Release → execution (confirm operations, issue materials) → technical completion → settle to CO (cost center, order, or asset).

Data plane and control plane

Data plane: Equipment (EQUI), functional location (IFLO), order (AUFK, AFKO, AFPO for operations), notification (QMEL), maintenance plan and call objects. Control plane: Order type, maintenance plan type, settlement rules, and PM customizing (SPRO). See Module 1.

Deployment and integration

Deployment: On‑premise and S/4HANA Cloud. Integration: Materials and procurement from MM; costs to CO (and FI for capitalization); link to QM (defect notification) and PP (equipment as work center).

Example: Corrective maintenance — from notification to order and cost

Operator reports a defect on pump EQUI-1000 (functional location LINE-A).

  1. Notification (IW21): Create notification: short text “Pump vibration,” object EQUI-1000. Notification 8000000123 is saved. No cost yet.
  2. Maintenance order (IW31 or from notification): Create order for EQUI-1000. Add operation “Repair pump” (work center, duration). Add material component: spare part PUMP-SEAL, 1 PC. Order 1000000456. Release order.
  3. Execution: Technician confirms operation (time); spare part is issued (MIGO or from order). Costs (labor, material) post to order. Technical completion: Order completed. Settlement (KO8G or automatic): Order costs settle to cost center 1000 (repair cost) or to asset (if capitalization).

Result: Defect documented; repair cost in CO (and optionally on asset in FI).

Important T-codes and concepts

Orders: IW31 (create), IW32 (change), IW33 (display). Notification: IW21 (create), IW22 (change). Plans: IP30 (maintenance plan). Concepts: Functional location vs equipment; notification vs order; settlement to cost center or asset.

📝 Module Quiz

Q1: PM manages:

  • A) Equipment and maintenance orders
  • B) Only sales
  • C) Only procurement
  • D) Only payroll
Correct: A
PM = Plant Maintenance: equipment, functional locations, orders.

Q2: EQUI is:

  • A) Sales document
  • B) Equipment master
  • C) Purchase order
  • D) G/L account
Correct: B
EQUI = equipment; IFLO = functional location.

Q3: PM order costs post to:

  • A) Only SD
  • B) Only MM
  • C) CO (and FI)
  • D) Only QM
Correct: C
Maintenance orders are costed in CO; settlement can go to cost center or asset.

Q4: A maintenance notification is typically used to:

  • A) Post vendor invoice
  • B) Report a defect or request for maintenance (then convert to order)
  • C) Create sales order
  • D) Run MRP
Correct: B
Notification documents the defect; order executes the work and collects cost.
