Module 34: Global Trade Services (GTS)

For beginners: When you ship or receive goods across borders, you must comply with customs (duties, declarations, licenses) and trade regulations (export control, sanctioned parties, preferences). SAP GTS (Global Trade Services) handles this: it checks sales and purchase documents against customs and compliance rules, supports customs declarations, export control (e.g. dual-use goods), and sanctioned-party list screening (so you don’t do business with blocked entities). GTS can run embedded in S/4HANA or as a separate GTS system that connects to S/4HANA (or ECC). Key terms: customs declaration, export control, SPL (sanctioned party list), preference (e.g. tariff preference for origin).

SAP GTS addresses customs and global trade compliance. It integrates with SD (sales, delivery), MM (purchasing, goods movement), and FI. Companies that trade internationally use GTS to avoid penalties, speed up customs clearance, and manage export and embargo controls.

Purpose and scope

GTS covers: Customs Management: Customs declarations (import/export), duty calculation, customs tariff (e.g. HS codes), customs procedures (e.g. inward/outward processing), and communication with customs authorities (e.g. ATLAS, AEP). Export Control: Legal control (e.g. dual-use, military), classification (ECCN, control list), license determination, and blocking of documents if export is not allowed. Sanctioned Party List (SPL) screening: Check business partners (customer, vendor, etc.) and optionally addresses against official lists (e.g. OFAC, EU sanctions); block or alert if hit. Preference and origin: Determine origin of goods (e.g. for free-trade agreements); support preference certificates and duty savings. Risk management: Customs and trade risk (e.g. classification risk). GTS can be used for both outbound (export) and inbound (import) and for intra-EU or non-EU trade depending on configuration.

In plain terms: Before you ship to a customer, GTS can run a sanctioned-party check: is the customer or ship-to address on a government block list? If yes, the system can block the delivery and alert compliance. For customs, when the truck leaves your warehouse for another country, a customs declaration (e.g. export declaration) is required; GTS can create and submit it using data from the delivery and material (e.g. HS code, weight, value).

Features in detail

  • Customs declaration: Declaration is created from delivery or shipment (reference to material, quantity, value, origin, HS code). Can be export, import, or transit. GTS calculates duties and taxes where applicable; declaration can be sent to customs (e.g. via EDI or national system). Status (released, cleared) is tracked. Supports multiple countries and procedures.
  • Export control: Material is classified (e.g. ECCN, control list). When a sales order or delivery is created, GTS checks whether the combination of material, destination country, and (optionally) end user requires a license or is prohibited. If license required, document can be blocked until license is entered or exception is approved. Prevents illegal exports.
  • SPL screening: When you create or change a business partner (or at order/delivery), GTS runs screening: compare partner name and address to sanctioned lists. If match (hit), system can block the transaction or require compliance release. Lists are updated (e.g. from official sources); screening can be real-time or batch.
  • Preference and origin: Origin of material (e.g. “EU,” “US”) is stored and used for preference determination (e.g. EU–UK FTA). Preference certificate (e.g. EUR.1) can be generated. Reduces duty when conditions are met.
  • Integration with SD/MM: GTS is called from SD (order, delivery) and MM (purchasing, GR) for checks and document creation. Blocking can prevent delivery or GR until compliance is resolved. Customs documents and status can flow back to S/4HANA.

Data plane and control plane

Data plane: GTS stores customs declarations, screening results, licenses, and classification data. It reads master data (material, business partner) and transaction data (order, delivery, PO) from S/4HANA via integration. GTS has its own repository for trade documents and compliance results. Control plane: GTS configuration: legal control settings, customs procedures, SPL list sources and update jobs, preference rules, and integration settings (connection to S/4HANA, customs systems). See Module 1.

Deployment and integration

Deployment: GTS can run embedded in S/4HANA (same system) or as a separate GTS system (e.g. central GTS for multiple ERPs). S/4HANA Cloud may offer GTS capabilities depending on scope. Integration: With S/4HANA (or ECC): RFC or API to read and write; delivery or order is sent to GTS for checks and declaration creation; block or release is sent back. With customs authorities: EDI or national interfaces (e.g. ATLAS in Germany). With list providers: SPL lists are loaded into GTS periodically or on demand.

Example: SPL screening at order and export declaration at delivery

SPL at order: Sales order is created for customer “ABC Trading,” ship-to country XY. GTS runs SPL screening on sold-to and ship-to. “ABC Trading” matches a name on the sanctioned list (e.g. fuzzy match). GTS returns “hit”; order is blocked. Compliance officer reviews: false positive (different entity). Officer releases the block with comment. Order is released and can be delivered.

Export declaration at delivery: Delivery 8000000500 is created for export to country Z. GTS receives delivery data (material, quantity, value, incoterms). Material has HS code and origin in master. GTS creates export declaration; declaration is submitted to customs (e.g. via national gateway). Customs clears the declaration. GTS updates status “cleared”; delivery can proceed (e.g. goods issue). Without GTS, you would create declarations manually or in a separate customs system.

Key GTS concepts

Term / ComponentPurpose
Customs declarationOfficial document for import/export/transit; created from delivery/shipment; submitted to customs.
Export controlCheck if material/destination/end user requires license or is prohibited; block if not compliant.
SPL (Sanctioned Party List)Screening of business partners against official block lists; block or alert on hit.
Preference / originOrigin of goods for free-trade agreements; preference certificates; duty savings.
HS codeHarmonized System code (tariff number) for customs classification of goods.
Relationship to SD and MM: SD creates sales orders and deliveries; MM creates purchase orders and goods movements. GTS does not replace them—it adds compliance checks and customs documents. Configure GTS to run at the right point (e.g. order save, delivery creation) so that non-compliant transactions are blocked before execution.

Key tables and T-codes

GTS uses its own data model (customs documents, screening log, licenses, etc.). Integration with S/4HANA uses IDocs, RFC, or APIs. T-codes and Fiori apps depend on GTS release (e.g. customs declaration workbench, screening log, export control checks). See SAP Help for GTS.

📝 Module Quiz

Q1: What is SPL screening in GTS?

  • A) Only material classification
  • B) Checking business partners (and optionally addresses) against sanctioned party lists; block or alert on hit
  • C) Only pricing
  • D) Only production
Correct: B
SPL screening prevents doing business with sanctioned entities by comparing partners to official lists.

Q2: Export control in GTS is used to:

  • A) Only calculate price
  • B) Check if material/destination/end user requires a license or is prohibited; block document if not compliant
  • C) Only post to FI
  • D) Only create purchase order
Correct: B
Export control ensures you do not export controlled goods without a license or to prohibited destinations.

Q3: GTS integrates most with which modules?

  • A) Only PP
  • B) SD (sales, delivery) and MM (purchasing, GR) for customs and compliance checks
  • C) Only QM
  • D) Only PM
Correct: B
GTS is triggered from SD and MM documents for export/import and screening.

Q4: Customs declaration in GTS is typically created from:

  • A) Only G/L posting
  • B) Delivery or shipment (material, quantity, value, HS code, origin)
  • C) Only cost center
  • D) Only asset
Correct: B
Declaration uses delivery/shipment data and material master (e.g. HS code, origin) to create and submit the customs document.
