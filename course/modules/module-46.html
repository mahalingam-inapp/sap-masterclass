Module 46: How a Typical Enterprise Uses SAP for Daily Operations

For beginners: So far you have learned each SAP module on its own. In reality, one company runs one (or a few) SAP systems, and many people use them at once: sales creates orders, warehouse ships, finance records payments, production builds products. This chapter shows how a typical enterprise uses these modules together in daily operations—with concrete examples. You will follow one customer order from entry to cash, one purchase from requisition to payment, and see how production, warehouse, HR, and finance fit in. The goal is to see SAP as a single operating system for the business, not as separate “silos.”

We use a single, consistent example company throughout: “Northgate Manufacturing”—a mid-size company that makes industrial equipment, sells to business customers, runs one plant and one distribution warehouse, and uses S/4HANA (with Fiori) for core ERP. They also use SAP SuccessFactors for HR, Concur for travel, and Ariba for some procurement. The examples are simplified but realistic; every step maps to modules you have studied.

The typical enterprise: Northgate Manufacturing

Profile: Northgate has one company code (legal entity), one plant (production), one storage location at the warehouse, and about 500 employees. They sell finished goods (equipment and spare parts) to B2B customers. Production uses BOMs and work centers; purchasing buys raw materials and MRO (maintenance, repair, operations) supplies. Finance runs month-end close; HR uses SuccessFactors (employee data syncs to S/4HANA for cost center and payroll-related data where needed).

  • Core systems: S/4HANA (FI, CO, MM, SD, PP, PM, EWM at warehouse), SuccessFactors (HR), Concur (T&E), Ariba (supplier catalogs and some POs).
  • Key roles: Sales (order entry, pricing), Warehouse (picking, shipping), Production (orders, confirmations), Procurement (POs, invoices), Finance (AR, AP, G/L, close), HR (SuccessFactors + payroll interface).

Example 1: One customer order from entry to cash

We follow one order through the company. This is the classic order-to-cash (O2C) process; every step is done by a person (or automation) using SAP.

Order 80001234 — “Customer Acme Corp, 10 units of product P-100, delivery in 5 days”

SD Monday 09:00 — Sales creates the order. The sales rep enters the order in Fiori “Manage Sales Orders” (or VA01): customer Acme Corp, material P-100, quantity 10, requested delivery date in 5 days. System checks material availability (ATP), applies pricing (from condition records), and may run credit check (FSCM if active). Order 80001234 is saved. Modules: SD (order), optionally FSCM (credit).
Data plane (tables): VBAK (sales order header), VBAP (sales order item), KONV (pricing conditions). ATP checks stock tables (e.g. MARD, MARC).
GRC: User authorization checked (order creation); segregation of duties (SoD) between order entry and release; audit log records who created the order and when.
PP / MM Monday 10:00 — Planning and production (if make-to-order or shortage). If P-100 is make-to-stock, warehouse may already have stock. If not, MRP (MD04 or planning run) creates a planned order or triggers production. Production orders (PP) are released; components are issued (MM), work is confirmed (PP), and finished goods are received into stock (MM). For this example, assume P-100 is in stock at the warehouse. Modules: PP, MM (goods movements, inventory).
Data plane (tables): AFPO/AFKO (production order), RESB (reservations), MSEG/MKPF (material document for issue/receipt), MARD (stock). CO postings: COEP (line items).
GRC: Release of production order may require approval workflow; goods movements are critical—SoD between person who orders and person who posts GR; change logs on orders support audit.
EWM / MM Tuesday — Warehouse prepares delivery. Outbound delivery is created (from the order or by batch job). In EWM, a wave picks the 10 units from storage; warehouse worker confirms pick and pack. Goods issue is posted: inventory for P-100 decreases (MM), and the delivery document is updated. Shipment is handed to the carrier (TM may be used for freight order and tendering). Modules: SD (delivery), EWM (or WM), MM (goods issue), optionally TM.
Data plane (tables): LIKP (delivery header), LIPS (delivery item); MSEG/MKPF (goods issue material document); MARD (stock reduction). EWM: /SCWM/ tables for waves and tasks.
GRC: SoD between order entry and goods issue; delivery and stock changes are logged for inventory audits; export compliance (GTS) may block shipment if not cleared.
SD + FI Tuesday afternoon — Billing. Billing clerk runs billing (VF01 or Fiori “Manage Billing Documents”) for delivery 8000000567. System creates billing document 9000000789: invoice to Acme Corp for 10 × unit price + tax. At the same time, FI automatically receives a posting: Debit Accounts Receivable (customer), Credit Revenue (and tax). No double entry—one billing event, one FI document. Invoice is sent to the customer (e-mail or portal). Modules: SD (billing), FI (AR + revenue posting).
Data plane (tables): VBRK/VBRP (billing document); BKPF (FI doc header), BSEG (FI doc line items—AR, revenue, tax); ACDOCA (S/4HANA universal journal).
GRC: Billing is a critical control point—SoD between delivery and billing; revenue recognition controls (RAR if used); audit trail links SD billing to FI document for statutory and internal audit.
CO Same moment — Cost of sales. When billing posts revenue, cost of sales can post to CO (from material valuation or CO-PA). Profitability analysis (CO-PA) shows revenue and cost for this order/customer. Modules: CO (cost of sales, CO-PA).
Data plane (tables): COEP (CO line items), ACDOCA (universal journal); CO-PA: CE1xxx/CE4xxx (profitability segments). Material valuation: MBEW, etc.
GRC: Cost and revenue in one place support management and statutory reporting; access to CO-PA often restricted for confidentiality; period lock prevents backdated changes.
FI Next week — Customer pays. Bank receives Acme’s payment (e.g. 15,000 EUR). Finance posts the incoming payment in FI (or via bank statement automation): allocate payment to invoice 9000000789. Accounts receivable line item is cleared; cash increases. Order is financially closed. Modules: FI (AR clearing, bank).
Data plane (tables): BKPF/BSEG (payment document); BSID/BSAD (AR line items—cleared); bank subledger tables; ACDOCA updated with clearing.
GRC: SoD between posting AR and releasing payments; payment run often requires dual control; bank reconciliation is a key control; audit trail for cash and AR.

Summary: One order touched SD (order, delivery, billing), MM (inventory/stock), EWM (warehouse), FI (AR, revenue, payment), and CO (cost of sales, profitability). Nobody re-keyed the same data; each step used the same document flow and integration you saw in Module 31.

Example 2: From “we need material” to “vendor is paid”

This is procure-to-pay (P2P). We start from a shortage or a planned requirement and end when the vendor is paid.

Purchase of raw material R-200 — “Production needs 500 kg for next week”

MM / PP Requirement. MRP (or manual requisition) creates a purchase requisition: 500 kg of R-200, plant 1000, delivery by next Monday. Requisition is in MM; it can be converted to a purchase order or sourced via Ariba if the company uses it for this category. Modules: MM (requisition), optionally Ariba.
Data plane (tables): EBAN (purchase requisition), EKET (schedule lines). MRP writes planned orders (PLAF, etc.).
GRC: Requisition approval workflow; SoD between requester and buyer; access control by purchasing org/plant.
MM Purchase order. Buyer creates PO 4500012345: vendor “Steel Corp,” material R-200, 500 kg, price per kg, delivery address (plant/storage location). PO is sent to the vendor (e-mail, Ariba Network, or EDI). Modules: MM (PO).
Data plane (tables): EKKO (PO header), EKPO (PO item), EKET (schedule lines). Release strategy tables if used.
GRC: PO release strategy (value/authority); SoD between requisition, PO creation, and GR; GTS import compliance check before PO release.
MM Goods receipt. On Monday, 500 kg of R-200 arrives. Warehouse (or receiving) posts goods receipt in MIGO (or Fiori): against PO 4500012345, 500 kg received. Inventory increases; GR/IR (goods receipt/invoice receipt) account is used until the invoice is posted. Modules: MM (goods receipt, inventory).
Data plane (tables): MKPF/MSEG (material document); MARD (stock); EKBE (PO history). GR/IR clearing in FI (BSIS/BSAS or ACDOCA).
GRC: SoD between person who ordered and person who posts GR; inventory controls and cycle count; audit trail for receipt.
MM + FI Invoice verification. Vendor sends invoice for 500 kg × price. Accounts payable clerk enters it in MIRO (or Fiori “Manage Vendor Invoices”): references PO and GR. System performs three-way match (PO, GR, invoice). If OK, posting is done: Debit expense/inventory (and tax), Credit vendor (AP). FI document is created automatically; GR/IR is cleared. Modules: MM (invoice verification), FI (AP + G/L).
Data plane (tables): RSEG (invoice receipt); BKPF/BSEG (FI document); BSID/BSAD (AP line items); ACDOCA; EKBE updated.
GRC: Three-way match (PO, GR, invoice) is a key control; SoD between invoice entry and payment; duplicate invoice checks; audit trail for AP.
FI Payment. At payment run (e.g. weekly), finance runs the payment program: selects open items for vendors and payment method (e.g. bank transfer). System generates payment file; bank executes. FI posts: Debit vendor (AP), Credit bank. Vendor line item is cleared. Modules: FI (AP, bank).
Data plane (tables): BKPF/BSEG (payment doc); BSIK/BSAK (AP cleared); bank account tables; payment medium (e.g. PAYR).
GRC: Payment run approval; dual control for release; SoD between invoice verification and payment execution; bank reconciliation and fraud controls.

Summary: MM (requisition, PO, GR, invoice verification) and FI (AP, payment). CO gets the cost center from the PO for reporting. If Ariba is used, the PO may originate or be approved there and then sync to S/4HANA.

Example 3: Production and warehouse in the same day

Northgate’s plant and warehouse work side by side: production consumes raw materials and produces finished goods; warehouse receives, stores, and ships.

A typical day at the plant and warehouse

  • Production (PP + MM): Planner releases production orders for the week. Shop floor confirms operations (labor, machine time); components are issued to the order (MM goods issue—inventory decreases). When the order is completed, goods receipt for finished product is posted (MM—inventory increases). CO collects labor and machine costs (cost center, activity types) and settles to the order; product cost is updated. If a machine breaks, a notification is created in PM and a maintenance order is generated; after repair, order is completed and costs go to cost center or order.
  • Warehouse (EWM + MM): Inbound: trucks arrive with raw materials or purchased parts. Receiving posts goods receipt (MM/EWM); putaway tasks move stock to bins. Outbound: as in Example 1, sales orders create outbound deliveries; waves and pick tasks are executed; goods issue is posted when the shipment leaves. Stock levels in MM and EWM stay in sync.
  • Quality (QM): If an inspection is required (e.g. incoming inspection for R-200), an inspection lot is created at goods receipt. Quality inspects and records results; usage decision (accept/reject) drives whether stock is usable or blocked. Same idea for in-process or final inspection in production.

Summary: PP (orders, confirmations, settlement), MM (all movements), EWM (warehouse tasks), PM (maintenance when needed), QM (inspection and release), CO (costs and product costing).

Data plane (tables): PP: AFKO/AFPO, AUFK; MM: MKPF/MSEG, MARD; EWM: /SCWM/; PM: AUFK (orders), QM: QALS, QAVE (inspection/usage); CO: COEP, ACDOCA.
GRC: SoD between planning and execution; production and inventory changes logged; quality blocks enforce compliance; maintenance and cost postings support asset and cost controls.

Example 4: Supporting operations — HR, travel, and month-end

Daily operations are not only “order, produce, ship, pay.” People, travel, and period-end close are also part of how the enterprise uses SAP.

HR and travel

SuccessFactors: Hiring, onboarding, goals, performance, and learning are in the cloud. Employee master data (e.g. person number, cost center, org unit) can replicate to S/4HANA so that when time is recorded or costs are allocated, the right cost center is used. Concur: Employees book travel and submit expenses in Concur. Approved expense reports can create postings in S/4HANA (via CPI): debit expense account (and employee receivable if applicable), credit AP or bank. So “daily operations” for a sales rep include Concur for a trip and S/4HANA for the order they closed.

Data plane (tables): Replicated employee/cost center data in S/4HANA; FI: BKPF/BSEG, ACDOCA for expense postings; AP for travel vendor or employee clearing.
GRC: Expense approval workflow; SoD between requester and approver; policy compliance (travel, delegation); audit trail for T&E and HR data.

Month-end close (Finance)

At period-end, finance runs the books. FI: Recurring entries, accruals, depreciation (FI-AA), bank reconciliation. Subledgers (AR, AP) are closed to the G/L; financial statements are generated. CO: Cost center allocations (KO8G or equivalent), internal order settlement, profit center reporting. If the company uses Central Finance, source systems replicate to central and group reporting runs there. If they use RAR, revenue recognition runs and posts contract assets/liabilities. Reporting: BW/4HANA or SAC may pull data from S/4HANA for management dashboards and statutory reports. GRC: Access recertification and control checks may run as part of the close or compliance cycle (SoD, process controls, audit readiness).

Data plane (tables): FI/CO close: BKPF/BSEG, ACDOCA, COEP; asset depreciation: ANEP; replications to Central Finance; GRC: AGR* (roles), audit logs, control run results.
GRC: Period lock prevents prior-period postings; access recertification (GRC AC) and SoD checks; process control monitoring (GRC PC); audit trail for close and reporting.

In plain terms: “Modules” are not separate buildings—they are different areas of the same system. When the warehouse posts “goods issue for delivery 8000000567,” that one action updates SD (delivery status), MM (inventory), and later FI (when billing posts revenue and cost). So the enterprise runs one integrated process; the course split it into modules so you could learn each part clearly.

Integration in practice: one action, many effects

To reinforce how “one action” propagates:

  • Posting a vendor invoice (MIRO): One click can create an FI document (AP + expense/inventory), update the vendor balance, and (if cost center is on the PO) send cost to CO. GR/IR is cleared. No separate “FI entry” needed.
  • Creating a billing document (VF01): One action creates the customer invoice (SD), posts AR and revenue (FI), and can post cost of sales (CO). Document flow links order → delivery → billing → FI document.
  • Confirming a production order: Confirmation posts labor and machine time to CO (cost center); goods issue and goods receipt move inventory (MM) and update the order. When the order is settled, cost flows to the finished product or to a profit center.

This is why master data and configuration matter: the right cost center, account, and material type ensure that automatic postings hit the correct place in FI and CO.

📝 Module Quiz

Q1: When the warehouse posts “goods issue” for an outbound delivery, what happens next in SAP?

  • A) Only SD is updated
  • B) Inventory decreases (MM), delivery status updates (SD); later billing will post AR and revenue (FI) and cost of sales (CO)
  • C) Only FI is updated
  • D) Only PM is updated
Correct: B
Goods issue is one event that updates MM and SD; the subsequent billing step creates the FI and CO postings.

Q2: In procure-to-pay, who typically creates the FI document (AP + expense) when the vendor invoice is received?

  • A) Only the vendor
  • B) The AP clerk (or automated process) when they post invoice verification (MIRO); the system creates the FI document automatically
  • C) Only SD
  • D) Only PP
Correct: B
Invoice verification in MM triggers the FI posting; no separate manual FI entry is needed for standard P2P.

Q3: Why does a “typical enterprise” use several modules in one day?

  • A) Each department has a separate SAP system
  • B) One SAP system supports many processes; sales, warehouse, production, and finance all use the same system but different transactions and apps (SD, MM, PP, FI, etc.)
  • C) Only one module is used per day
  • D) Modules are only for reporting
Correct: B
The enterprise runs one integrated system; different roles use different parts (modules) of it.

Q4: In the order-to-cash example, which module is responsible for posting “Accounts Receivable” and “Revenue” when the customer is billed?

  • A) Only SD
  • B) FI—the posting is triggered by SD billing (VF01); FI stores the AR and revenue and tax accounts
  • C) Only PP
  • D) Only MM
Correct: B
SD billing triggers the posting; the accounting document (AR, revenue) is created and stored in FI.
